# Purpose

This document defines the canonical vocabulary used throughout Opportunity OS.

Every document, API, database schema, event, prompt, dashboard, and codebase component must use these definitions consistently.

Each term has exactly one meaning.

New terminology should be added here before being used elsewhere.

# Scope

The glossary covers four domains:

1.  Business Domain

2.  Intelligence Domain

3.  Data Acquisition Domain

4.  Engineering Domain

These definitions are technology-independent.

# Business Domain

## Opportunity

A validated business hypothesis generated from one or more customer problems and supported by measurable evidence.

An Opportunity is the primary business object presented to users.

It is not merely an AI-generated idea; it is a structured recommendation backed by data, trends, and deterministic scoring.

## Customer Problem

A recurring challenge experienced by users.

Customer Problems exist independently of any platform.

Examples:

- Users struggle to organize AI prompts.

- Developers spend excessive time debugging deployments.

Customer Problems are the fundamental subject of analysis.

## Market

A collection of related Customer Problems.

Examples:

- AI Productivity

- Creator Economy

- Developer Tools

- Education Technology

Markets contain Opportunities.

## User Segment

A group of users sharing similar characteristics and problems.

Examples:

- Startup founders

- Product managers

- Students

- Designers

## ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

The User Segment most likely to adopt the proposed solution first.

Each Opportunity should identify one recommended ICP.

# Intelligence Domain

## Pain Point

A structured interpretation of a Customer Problem extracted from a single Canonical Content item.

One piece of content may contain multiple Pain Points.

Pain Points are generated by the AI Analysis Platform.

## Pain Point Cluster

A semantic grouping of related Pain Points.

Clusters reduce redundancy by representing a recurring problem rather than individual mentions.

## Trend

A measurable change in a Pain Point Cluster over time.

Trend examples:

- Emerging

- Growing

- Stable

- Declining

A Trend describes observed behavior rather than future prediction.

## Evidence Quote

A verbatim excerpt from Canonical Content supporting a Pain Point or Opportunity.

Evidence Quotes provide explainability and traceability.

They preserve the original wording whenever possible.

## Opportunity Score

A deterministic numerical assessment of an Opportunity.

The score is calculated by software rather than directly generated by an AI model.

## Confidence Score

A numerical estimate of confidence in an AI-generated interpretation.

Confidence reflects evidence quality, model certainty, and consistency.

Confidence is not a replacement for evidence.


# Data Acquisition Domain

## Data Acquisition Framework

The reusable framework responsible for acquiring external information and delivering immutable Raw Content into the platform.

The framework is independent of business logic.

## Connector

A software component that acquires information from one external source.

Connectors never perform:

- normalization

- AI analysis

- scoring

- business logic

## Pull Connector

A Connector that actively requests information from an external system.

Examples:

- Reddit search

- Xiaohongshu search

- Douyin search

## Push Connector

A Connector that receives externally generated events.

Examples:

- Webhooks

- Slack events

- GitHub events

## Import Connector

A Connector that processes user-provided datasets.

Examples:

- CSV

- JSON

- Support exports

- Interview transcripts

## Connector Registry

The service responsible for discovering, validating, and registering Connectors.

## Connector Runner

The orchestration component responsible for executing Connectors, handling retries, recording execution metrics, and publishing acquisition events.

## Raw Content

The immutable payload returned by a Connector.

Raw Content is stored exactly as received.

It is never modified.

## Normalization Service

The Intelligence Platform component responsible for transforming Raw Content into Canonical Content.

Normalization is deterministic.

No semantic interpretation occurs during normalization.

## Canonical Content

The normalized, platform-independent representation of customer information.

All downstream services consume Canonical Content.

Canonical Content is immutable.

# Engineering Domain

## Domain Model

The conceptual representation of the business entities and relationships within Opportunity OS.

The Domain Model is independent of database implementation.

## Event

An immutable record describing something that has already happened.

Events communicate facts between services.

Events are append-only.

## Event Envelope

The standardized metadata surrounding every Event.

The envelope contains identifiers, timestamps, correlation IDs, version information, and the Event payload.

## Correlation ID

A unique identifier linking all Events generated during a single workflow.

It enables complete traceability across services.

## Aggregate

A consistency boundary within the Domain Model.

Aggregate Roots own related entities and enforce business rules.


# AI Domain

## AI Analysis Platform

The platform responsible for semantic interpretation.

Responsibilities include:

- Pain Point extraction

- summarization

- clustering assistance

- classification

- hypothesis generation

It never performs deterministic business calculations.

## Prompt Template

A version-controlled instruction used to invoke an AI model.

Prompt Templates are treated as software artifacts.

## Embedding

A vector representation of text used for semantic similarity and clustering.

Embeddings are implementation details and are not exposed directly to end users.

## AI Workflow

A repeatable sequence of AI-powered processing steps.

Examples:

- Pain Point Extraction

- Cluster Naming

- Opportunity Hypothesis Generation

Each workflow is versioned.

## Provenance

Metadata describing how an AI-generated object was produced.

Typical fields include:

- model

- provider

- prompt version

- timestamp

- source evidence

Provenance supports explainability and reproducibility.
